## 2. Mechanism — the "why" (Section B)

### 2.1 Mines vs UXO — definitional and physical distinction
- **Mine**: a munition designed to detonate on contact/pressure/target signature. Victim-activated by design;
  the casualty mechanism is the *designed* function of the weapon (LM2025 casualty-cause taxonomy:
  "victim-activated improvised landmines" 2,077 casualties in 2024 vs "manufactured antipersonnel mines" 1,540).
  URL: https://icblcmc.org/assets/reports/Landmine-Monitors/LMM2025/Downloads/Landmine-Monitor-2025-Final-Online.pdf
- **UXO / ERW**: ordnance that *failed to detonate* when used — air-dropped bombs, artillery shells, mortars,
  rockets, cluster submunitions. Here the casualty mechanism is an *accident* of residual explosive,
  not a designed victim trigger. Consequence: UXO is not targeted at victims, it is merely left behind,
  so it is dispersed over civilian terrain (fields, wells, roads, housing) rather than laid in patterns.
- Engineering consequence (from GICHD): a mine is a *dense, patterned* threat; UXO is a *diffuse* threat.
  "A patterned minefield is one in which mines are laid in rows or clusters ... When one mine is detonated
  or otherwise located, it can indicate the location of the remaining mines." In non-patterned minefields
  "when one mine has been located ... its location cannot be used to determine the location of other mines".
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/ (GICHD, "A Study of Mechanical Application in Demining", Ch.3)
  => Clearance economics differ fundamentally: for patterned minefields, a *single* detection collapses the
  search space; for UXO/non-patterned, every square metre must be searched exhaustively.

### 2.2 Why the casualty mix has shifted toward UXO in some regions
- LM2025 (data year 2024): ERW caused **56% of all child casualties**; children = **75% (945)** of ERW casualties
  where age was known. Improvised mines 283 (17%), AP mines 238 (14%).
  URL: https://icblcmc.org/assets/reports/Landmine-Monitors/LMM2025/Downloads/Landmine-Monitor-2025-Final-Online.pdf
- Interpretation (mechanism, not speculation): UXO is dispersed over exactly the surfaces children use
  (playgrounds, fields, water access) and is *not* marked, whereas AP mines are concentrated in
  defensive belts that can be mapped and signed.
- Corroborating: Ukraine's contamination is dominated by ERW rather than AP mines, and the CSIS analysis
  attributes the record farmland contamination to Russian use of mines *and* ERW together.
  URL: https://www.csis.org/analysis/demining-ukraines-farmland-progress-adaptation-and-needs

### 2.3 ERW as an area-denial and livelihood instrument (not only a casualty source)
- Tony Blair Institute economic-impact assessment for Ukraine: contamination across Ukrainian territory
  "suppresses the country's GDP by USD 11.2 billion annually or approximately 5.6 percent of Ukraine's GDP
  in 2021"; annual agricultural exports are "USD 4.3 billion lower in value than they would be absent
  this contamination."
  URL: https://www.csis.org/analysis/demining-ukraines-farmland-progress-adaptation-and-needs
- Ukraine action-plan framing: 208,000 ha returned to farmers in 2023 is "theoretically capable of producing
  nearly one million metric tons of wheat valued at USD 225 million on international markets"
  (Minister of Economy Yulia Svyridenko).
  URL: https://www.csis.org/analysis/demining-ukraines-farmland-progress-adaptation-and-needs
- So the human toll of ERW has two channels: (a) direct casualties, (b) denial of the means of production.
  Clearance is therefore not only a casualty-reduction instrument but a food-security instrument.

### 2.4 Access-to-water / access denial — mechanism and evidence
- Landmine Monitor 2025 records the *type of activity* at the moment of casualty (farming, collecting
  water, travelling, child play) — the Monitor's victim-survey instrument explicitly codes activity.
  LM2025 records that **90% of casualties are civilians** and 46% of civilian casualties are children,
  i.e. the casualty population is dominated by subsistence and domestic activity, not by combat.
  URL: https://icblcmc.org/assets/reports/Landmine-Monitors/LMM2025/Downloads/Landmine-Monitor-2025-Final-Online.pdf
  => the mechanism is: ERW denies access to land and water, so people must enter suspected areas
  to fetch water/fodder/wood, and that entry is what triggers the munition.
- Thai case (GICHD Ch.3, Case study 2): mine/UXO contamination affects **27 provinces** on Thailand's borders
  and impacts **530 communities**, "mostly poor rural villages".
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/

## 3. Clearance Engineering & Cost (Section C)

### 3.0 THE central engineering fact: almost all cleared ground contains no ordnance
- GICHD, "A Study of Mechanical Application in Demining" (Ch.1, Area reduction):
  "Clearance data gathered by the GICHD from **15 countries** suggests that of suspect areas cleared,
  **less than three per cent actually contained mines or other ordnance** (individual items of ordnance
  were allocated a ground coverage of one square metre). This suggests that **effective area reduction is
  the phase of demining where the greatest increases in efficiency can be made**. Thanks to their speed of
  operation, **machines are best placed to achieve such increases**. Manual survey cannot possibly cover
  the same area in as little time and should be used only where extremes of topography rule out the use
  of machines. **Dogs are good at area reduction, but are far more affected by such vagaries as weather,
  soil conditions and vegetation.**"
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
  => THE cost driver: >97% of cleared area is a false positive. Cost is dominated by *search*, not by
  *munitions*. This single number is the hinge of the entire report.

### 3.1 Method taxonomy (IMAS terminology)
- **Area reduction** = "the process through which the initial area indicated as contaminated is reduced to a
  smaller area" (IMAS definition quoted by GICHD). Machine-based area reduction is a *relatively new concept
  with no formal or fully understood techniques or procedures yet established*.
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- Machine families (GICHD Ch.1): **flails** ("the most common type of mechanical system currently on the
  market"; prototypes saw service in 1914-18; "More R&D funding has been invested in improving flails than
  in any other system"), **tillers**, **rollers**, **sifters**, **excavators**, plus **magnets** for
  removing metal contamination. Purpose-built single-pass machine "that can perform all of these functions
  in one or two passes of a suspect area ... **Such a machine does not exist at present**".
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- Flail strike taxonomy (the physics of the machine): **disruptive strike** (mine damaged so it "can be
  disrupted rather than detonated"), **detonation strike** (mine detonates on strike), **ejection strike**
  ("the mine being picked up and thrown clear of the flail unit ... commonly known as a throw-out").
  Throw-outs are "the principal contention among detractors of flail systems as a primary clearance method."
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
  => Engineering implication: a flail is a *detection-and-detonation* tool that mints NEW UXO
  (disrupted-but-live mines) unless followed by manual/MDD sweep. That is why flails are deployed as
  *ground preparation / area reduction* ahead of manual teams, not as stand-alone clearance.

### 3.2 Productivity per method (GICHD case-study data, Abkhazia / SE Asia; 20-day cycles)
| Method | Output per 20-day cycle | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Manual clearance (heavy metal contamination + heavy vegetation) | **1,600 m2** (= **80 m2/day**) | "clearance tasks, which only manual deminers can perform" |
| Manual + MDD (mine-detection dog) support | **3,000 m2** (= 150 m2/day) | **+87.5%** over manual |
| Mechanical area reduction (SDTT: slasher, mower, cultivator, roller, magnet) then MDD/manual | **22,400 m2** per 20 days + 22,400 m2 further mechanically reduced | **+1,400%** over manual; **+746.6%** over MDD+manual; **+2,100%** over manual counting the second block |

  Detail: "During 20-day periods the SDTT system was deployed to remove vegetation, break up ground to
  activate landmines and conduct multi-level investigation ... In addition, another 22,400 square metre
  area was mechanically reduced during the same period ... This totalled a **2,100 per cent increase in
  productivity over manual methods or a 1,120 per cent increase over MDD with manual deminer teams.**"
  Machine reality check: "machines do not work a full eight-hour day, rather **three to four hours each day**."
  Integrated team = **23 personnel** (1 commander, 1 deputy, 1 mech unit commander, 2 SDTT, 2 SDTT operators,
  4 MDD teams, 2 MDD supervisors, 8 mine dogs, 8 dog handlers, 1 supervisor, 6 deminers, 1 medic).
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- Also: MDD "cleared **300 square metres a day**" after corrective training; "Over a 20-day cycle, clearance
  of 3,000 square metres was typically achieved"; "This operation therefore **doubled production using half
  the number of deminers**." MDD "clearance results are best when MDD teams identify explosive-trace metal
  fragmentation no more than once every three to five metres."
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- Machine vegetation-cutting alone: HALO tractor "capable of cutting up to **3,400 square metres of land per
  day**, depending on the vegetation and terrain type", but the *actual* average was "**560 square metres** with
  an average working time of three hours per day" across 11 machines over 12 months, because deployment is
  rate-limited by the manual teams' progress. "the cutting rate of **500-600 square metres per day** reflects
  the rate of manual demining in the minefield, a limitation likely to apply to all types of non-intrusive
  vegetation cutters."
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
  => KEY: the pipeline is rate-limited by the slowest stage (manual). Machines alone do not raise total
  throughput unless the manual/MDD stage is also expanded or the search burden is reduced by area reduction.

### 3.3 Effect of machine ground preparation on manual productivity (HALO, n=190 lanes, 12 months)
- "Productivity improved in **all 191 instances** in which the deminer followed a cutting machine
  (Fig. 2). The average increase was **73.8 per cent** (+s.e. 3.3, range 7-200 per cent) with more than
  100 per cent achieved in 26 instances; the **median increase was 50 per cent**. In general terms,
  productivity increased by about 50 per cent when manual deminers followed a machine."
  Drivers of variance: (a) **metal contamination** in the ground (dominant); (b) **type of vegetation** —
  improvement was *smaller* in high "elephant-type grass" than in thick underbrush, "because the time costs
  of removing high grass are more substantial due to the small net clearance gained on each cut."
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- Breaching-lane tax: manual teams must first cut 4-m-wide breaching lanes. "if the breaching lanes
  represented **25 per cent** of the total area being demined and the effect of mechanically cutting
  vegetation was a **50 per cent** increase in productivity, then the net effect would be a **37.5 per cent**
  increase in productivity." Breaching lanes are a *subtraction* from the machine benefit.
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/

### 3.5 Cost drivers (the causal list, each with a GICHD or LM2025 anchor)
1. **False-positive rate of the search** (>97% of cleared area contains no ordnance) — GICHD, 15 countries.
2. **Metal contamination of soil** — determines how much of the manual/MDD pipeline must be spent on
   non-hazardous metal fragments; HALO found this to be the dominant determinant of manual productivity
   after vegetation removal (GICHD, n=190 lanes).
3. **Vegetation type and density** — grass vs underbrush changes the *net clearance gained per cut*
   (GICHD, HALO).
4. **Terrain and soil** — Abkhazia: "granite boulder with a thin layer of topsoil" plus extreme metal
   contamination made "daily manual clearance rates along the Gumista river rarely exceeded **five metres
   per lane**". Ground is only workable 10 months/year (frozen ground in winter).
5. **Patterned vs non-patterned minefields** — determines whether detection is log-efficient or exhaustive.
6. **Pipeline balance** — machine output (3,400 m2/day possible) is wasted if manual follow-up runs at
   80 m2/day; the system is limited by its slowest stage.
7. **Reporting/estimate error** — Thailand's true contamination was 3x the official estimate.
8. **FX, local manufacture, operator certification** — Ukraine: domestic production of demining equipment
   "can return up to 40 percent of the funding spent on production to Ukrainian businesses";
   certification build-out from 6 to 58 operators.
9. **Formal-sector vs ad-hoc clearance** — Ukraine's Prozorro open auction for demining services produced
   an **18% price drop**, "saving the state budget over UAH 44 million in its first 11 auctions".
   URL: https://www.csis.org/analysis/demining-ukraines-farmland-progress-adaptation-and-needs
10. **Cost of the residual area** — GICHD: machine purchase ~US$250,000 in some programs; "a cost-effective
   return on such an outlay is not always [guaranteed]" (GICHD Ch.1).

### 3.6 Detection technology: comparative performance (evidence-weighted)
- **MDD (mine-detection dogs)**: the only method that responds to *explosive residue* rather than metal.
  GICHD: "Area reduction is enhanced by the mine dog capability to focus on explosive vapours instead of
  non-hazardous metal scrap as located by metal detectors." Limitation: "MDD teams are not productive while
  waiting for manual deminers to clear vegetation with hand-held cutting tools"; MDD "still indicated a high
  number of shell and mortar fragments"; "dog interest in metal fragments could not be totally eradicated."
  And critically: "**Dogs are good at area reduction, but are far more affected by such vagaries as weather,
  soil conditions and vegetation.**" MDD best performance when explosive-trace fragmentation is no more than
  once every 3-5 metres.
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- **Metal detectors**: "inherent limited technical performance of metal detectors and hand-tools used to
  locate mines or UXO" is why some clearance tasks can *only* be done manually. Metal detectors find
  "thousands of metal splinters" in high-metal ground; "For MDDs, explosive molecules create extensive
  contamination over the area to be cleared" — so metal detection over-detects and dog detection
  over-detects in its own way (fragment response).
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- **Flail**: as above, a disruptive/detonation/ejection device. "By the nature of the flail operation, a
  flail cannot be used to clear an area of mines without creating the possibility of UXO" (the disrupted-mine
  problem); the accepted practice is machines *in support of* manual/MDD teams as **ground preparation
  systems**. "Currently, most mechanical mine clearance operations are in support of manual deminers and/or
  MDD teams as ground preparation systems."
  URL: https://www.gichd.org/
- **GPR**: no quantitative GPR performance figure located in the sources fetched so far -> routed to GAPS.
- **UAV/drone + AI detection**: funded in Ukraine via AWS partnerships with HALO Trust and Norwegian
  People's Aid for "AI-integrated innovations in landmine and ERW detection using drone imagery"; the
  Howard G. Buffet Foundation adapts tractors/bulldozers into remotely operated demining machines.
  URL: https://www.csis.org/analysis/demining-ukraines-farmland-progress-adaptation-and-needs
